Year-end predictions are a slightly silly genre and I enjoy them anyway. I don't think anything here is a bold call. It's more a list of things already in motion that I expect to matter more next year than most plans allow for.

## 1. GA4 stops being optional

Google Analytics 4 has been out for over a year. Universal Analytics still works, and Google hasn't said when it will stop. But nearly every new feature and every bit of Google's own messaging is going into GA4, and Universal Analytics was built for a world of cookies and page views that's disappearing.

My prediction is that 2022 is when the gap becomes impossible to ignore, and that businesses who haven't started will regret it. The main reason is history. GA4 doesn't import your old data. Whenever the switch happens, you'll want at least a year of GA4 data to compare against, and that clock only starts once it's installed and collecting properly.

"Installed" is doing a lot of work there. GA4 out of the box gives you basic events and not much else for ecommerce. Getting purchase data, product views, basket events and refunds in correctly takes real implementation work, and the event model is different enough that copying your old setup doesn't work. If I were writing one line in a 2022 plan, it would be: run GA4 alongside Universal Analytics, implemented properly, by spring.

## 2. Email open rates become meaningless

Apple's Mail Privacy Protection arrived with iOS 15 in September. For users who turn it on, Apple loads email content, including tracking pixels, in the background. Opens get recorded whether or not the person ever looked at the email.

So open rates are inflating, and in 2022 I expect plenty of businesses to notice their numbers have "improved" without anything having changed. Subject line tests judged on opens become unreliable. Re-engagement campaigns triggered by "hasn't opened in 90 days" stop working. The fix is to measure clicks and revenue, which is what we should have been doing anyway.

## 3. Page experience comes to desktop

Google has said the page experience ranking signal, which rolled out on mobile over the summer, will extend to desktop from February. I [didn't see much drama](/page-experience-rollout-first-look-20210629/) from the mobile rollout, and I don't expect much from this either. But many sites have only checked their mobile Core Web Vitals scores, so it's worth a look at the desktop report before February.

## 4. Third-party cookies still matter, for now

Chrome's cookie phase-out is currently planned for late 2023. I [wrote in July](/third-party-cookie-delay-extra-time-20210706/) about using the extra time, and my view hasn't changed. My prediction for 2022 is that most businesses won't use it, and the end of 2022 will feel like a rerun of the end of 2020. The ones who spend next year on consent, first-party data and better measurement will be in a much calmer place.

## 5. TikTok becomes a commerce channel

TikTok and Shopify expanded their partnership in August with a TikTok Shopping pilot, and shopping features keep appearing in the app. I'm not convinced it suits every brand; heritage and luxury brands in particular may find the tone hard to get right. But I think 2022 is when brands that ignored TikTok as "for teenagers" start to look at it seriously, if only because Facebook and Instagram are getting harder to measure.

## One I'm less sure about

I suspect supply chains won't fully settle in the first half of the year. Stock planning and honest delivery promises will still be a competitive advantage through spring, but I'm no economist, so take that one lightly.
