Last month, for the first time, the site did over a million sessions. A million-odd visits in one month, all of them coming to look at watches. It's a good number, and I don't want to be too cool about it; we marked it, and we should have.

The interesting thing about a milestone like this is what it took to reach it, and the answer is deeply mundane. It was years of compounding: content published and refined, technical SEO chipped away at, brand work that slowly got people searching for us by name, paid channels layered on top. Each of those is unshowy on its own and forgettable in any given week, and it took a few years of them to get here.

The thing I have to keep reminding myself is that a session is not a sale. A million of them measures attention, and a big round traffic figure can flatter you into thinking the job is done when all it really tells you is that a lot of people showed up. Whether that attention turned into orders, repeat purchases and revenue is a separate question, and a much less tidy number to put in front of people.

So I'm pleased, though trying not to read too much into it. The danger with a number this satisfying is that it quietly becomes the target, and you start chasing sessions for their own sake instead of the orders underneath them. The report I actually care about this month is the revenue one, and that is the next thing on my desk.
