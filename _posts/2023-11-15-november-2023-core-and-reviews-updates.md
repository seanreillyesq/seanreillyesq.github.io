Google started the November 2023 core update on the 2nd, then followed it with the November 2023 reviews update on the 8th. Both are rolling out at once, a fortnight after the October core update finished. That's three core updates since late August, plus the helpful content update in September. Search Console graphs have been hard to read all autumn.

The core update is the usual black box, and I've already written about [how I'd approach a core update drop](/august-2023-core-update-before-you-panic-20230829/). The reviews update is more interesting, because of what Google said alongside it.

## No more announcements

Google says this will be the last time it announces improvements to its reviews system. From now on they'll happen on a regular, ongoing basis without being flagged. It will still mention notable changes, such as expanding to more languages, but there won't be a named "reviews update" to point at.

For anyone who publishes reviews, buying guides or comparisons, that removes a useful signal. Until now, a drop on the day of a reviews update at least told you where to look. In future, movement in review content could happen at any time, and it'll be harder to tell why.

## It's not only product reviews

The system used to be called the product reviews system. Earlier this year Google dropped "product" from the name and widened it to cover reviews of services, businesses, destinations, media and pretty much anything that can be reviewed. The guidance is the same in spirit: Google wants reviews that show the reviewer actually used the thing, with original evidence like photos, measurements or comparisons, rather than a rewritten spec sheet.

## What it means for retailers

Most retailers don't think of themselves as review publishers, but plenty of them are:

- **Buying guides** ("best running shoes for beginners") that list the products you happen to stock
- **Comparison pages** between your own products
- **Blog posts** written to catch "X vs Y" or "is X worth it" searches

These are exactly the pages the reviews system looks at. A buying guide that only recommends what you stock, with no sign anyone has handled any of it, is going to struggle against a specialist who has tested the lot.

The good news is that retailers often have material others don't: returns data, customer questions, staff who have used the products, and of course customer reviews. A guide that says which boot runs small, which jacket people send back and why, and which product the staff actually buy for themselves is more useful than another list of products, and harder for anyone else to copy.

## Customer reviews on product pages

Separate from the reviews system, I think customer reviews on product pages matter more each year. They add fresh, specific text to pages that otherwise rarely change, and they answer the questions a buyer has in words the buyer uses. There's also been a lot of comment this year about forum content and Reddit threads turning up more often in results. People seem to want to hear from someone who owns the thing, and Google seems to be leaning that way too.

If you sell online and aren't collecting reviews consistently, I'd make that a priority before Christmas. Ask after delivery, make it easy, and don't filter out the critical ones.

## What I'd do this month

Nothing until both updates finish. Then look at your guide and comparison pages separately from the rest of the site in Search Console. If they've slipped, be honest about whether they show real experience. If they don't, either add it or rework them into something other than reviews.
