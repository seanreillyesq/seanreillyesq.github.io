Google's November core update started on 11 November and finished on 5 December. On 12 December, a week later, it started the December core update. Google says this one should take up to two weeks, so it'll probably land just before Christmas.

Two core updates back to back is unusual. Normally there are three or four a year with a few months between them, which at least gives you a chance to see where you've landed before the next one moves things again. If you're trying to read your Search Console data this month, I sympathise.

## Why two so close together?

Google's explanation is that it has several core systems, they're improved separately, and the December update involves a different one from November's. That's plausible, and it's a more candid answer than Google usually gives. The practical effect for site owners is the same though: overlapping ranking changes that are very hard to tell apart.

My guess is that some of this is a consequence of the March update. Folding the helpful content system into core ranking means the site-level quality judgement now moves with each core update. If Google wants to adjust that judgement, a core update is now the only way to ship it.

## The year in core updates

It's been a busy twelve months, and a hard one for a lot of small publishers.

- **March**, which I [wrote about at the time](/march-2024-core-update-spam-policies-20240312/), was the long one: about six weeks of rollout, the end of the separate helpful content system and three new spam policies.
- **August** ran from mid-August into early September. Google said it took account of feedback from smaller, independent sites that felt they'd been unfairly hit by the September 2023 helpful content update. Some of those sites recovered a little. Most of the ones I've seen discussed didn't get anywhere near back to where they were.
- **November and December** now, back to back.

Add in AI Overviews arriving in the UK in August, after their [US launch in May](/google-ai-overviews-launch-20240521/), and it's been a year where organic traffic numbers have been unusually hard to explain to anyone who doesn't follow this stuff closely.

## The site reputation abuse change

The change I think matters more than either core update came in between them, on 19 November. Google updated its site reputation abuse policy.

When the policy was introduced in March, it targeted third-party content published on a trusted site with little oversight from the host. A lot of publishers responded by arguing that they did have oversight. They edited the content, or owned part of the business producing it, or had a licensing agreement. The coupon sections, the "best credit cards" pages and the product review hubs on big newspaper domains carried on ranking.

The new wording closes that gap. Google now says that using third-party content to exploit a site's ranking signals is a violation regardless of whether the host is involved or has oversight. White-label arrangements, licensing deals and partial ownership don't make it acceptable. If the content is there because the host's domain ranks well, rather than because it fits what the site is about, it's in scope.

Google also says this is enforced through manual actions for now, not algorithmically. So it'll be applied site by site, and we'll find out who's been hit when the coupon sections start disappearing from results.

I'm broadly glad. For years, searching for a discount code or a product comparison has meant wading through newspaper subfolders that had nothing to do with journalism. Ordinary retailers and specialist sites were being outranked by brand names lent to someone else's content.

## What I'd do over the holidays

Very little, if you can resist. With two overlapping updates and a festive traffic pattern on top, December data is going to be a mess. I'd note the dates in your reporting, keep an eye out for manual actions in Search Console, and leave any real analysis until January, when the update has finished and traffic has gone back to normal.

If you host any third-party content on your domain, partner pages, sponsored reviews, a voucher section, look at it now in light of the new wording. That's the one thing here with a clear action attached.

I'll do a proper look at the December update in the new year once it's settled.
