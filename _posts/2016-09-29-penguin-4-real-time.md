After nearly two years of "soon", Google confirmed last Friday that Penguin 4.0 is rolling out. There were two headline changes in the announcement, and between them they change how the link spam problem works for most sites.

The first is that Penguin is now real-time and part of Google's core algorithm. Previously it ran as a periodic refresh. If you were hit, you cleaned up your links and then waited for the next refresh to see whether it had worked. The last proper refresh was at the end of 2014, which meant some sites had been sitting under a penalty they'd dealt with long ago, for the best part of two years, with nothing to do but wait. That's over. Changes to a site's links will now be picked up as pages are recrawled and reindexed.

The second is that Penguin is now "more granular". Google's wording is that it devalues spam by adjusting rankings based on spam signals, rather than affecting the ranking of the whole site. Put simply, a bad link is more likely to be ignored than to drag the entire domain down.

Google also said this is the last time it'll announce a Penguin update, because there's nothing left to announce. It just runs.

## What this means if you were penalised

If you did the clean-up work, removed or disavowed the bad links, and have been waiting for a refresh, you should see movement over the next few weeks as the rollout completes. Some people will get a pleasant surprise. Others will find that recovering from Penguin doesn't mean getting back to where they were, because those old links were propping up rankings they hadn't really earned, and they won't be counted again.

If you didn't do the clean-up, the pressure has eased a little. Granular devaluation is a gentler outcome than a sitewide demotion. I wouldn't read that as permission to stop caring.

## What it means for link building

This is where I think people will draw the wrong lesson. The tempting reading goes: Google now just ignores bad links, so there's no real downside to building them. Buy a few, and the worst case is they don't count.

I don't buy that, for three reasons:

- **You're still paying for nothing.** If the links are devalued, every pound and hour spent getting them is wasted, even if nothing bad happens.
- **Manual actions still exist.** Penguin is the algorithm. Google's webspam team can still hand out a manual penalty for an unnatural link pattern, and that's a much worse place to be.
- **Google gets faster feedback too.** Faster recovery also means faster feedback for anyone testing what they can get away with. I'd expect Google to keep tuning what counts as spam, and now it doesn't have to wait for a refresh to apply it.

The disavow file is still worth keeping. Google has said it remains useful, and if you inherit a site with a messy link history, I'd still review the profile and disavow the obvious junk rather than relying on Penguin to quietly sort it out.

## For an ecommerce site

On a retail site, the links that matter most tend to come from things you'd want to do anyway: press coverage, partnerships, being mentioned by people who write about the products you sell, being a useful reference in your category.

If anything, Penguin 4.0 makes life a bit easier for sites that have stayed clean. A competitor whose rankings rested on a pile of paid links may find those links quietly stop counting, and when that happens it won't be tied to an announced refresh date any more, so it'll be harder to spot.

I'm keeping an eye on our rankings across the main brand and model terms over the next few weeks while the rollout finishes, mostly out of curiosity about what happens to the sites around us.
