Most of the conversation about Google Shopping is about bidding. How to structure campaigns, how to split brand and generic, how much to bid on which product groups. That's all worth getting right, but I think the feed matters more, and it's usually the part that gets the least attention.

With text ads you choose the keywords you appear for. With Shopping you don't; Google decides when to show your products based mostly on what's in your feed. The title, the description, the category, the attributes. If the feed is thin or wrong, bidding can't fix it, because you're not showing up for the searches you should be.

## Titles first

The product title does the most work. Google leans on it heavily for matching, and it's the bit of text a shopper actually reads in the results.

Most feeds just pull the product name from the website, which is usually written for someone already on the product page. A model name on its own tells a shopper very little. Something closer to brand, product type, model, key attribute and size or colour works much better: "[Brand] [Model] Men's Hybrid Bike 2018, 19 inch, Grey". Front-load the words people search for, because titles get cut off in the results.

You don't have to change the titles on the website to do this. Most feed tools and platform extensions let you build the feed title from attributes, so the site can keep its own naming.

## The fields people leave blank

- **GTIN.** Google has required GTINs for products that have them since last year. Products with correct GTINs get matched to the right searches far more reliably, and missing ones will get you warnings or disapprovals in Merchant Center. If your suppliers give you barcodes, use them.
- **Brand and MPN.** Same logic, and brand at least is easy to fill from data you already have.
- **Google product category.** Use the most specific category from Google's taxonomy you can, not the top level.
- **Product type.** Your own category structure, which you can then use to organise campaigns.
- **Colour, size, gender, age group.** Required for apparel and shoes, useful for everything where they apply. For cycling kit and clothing this matters a lot.
- **Custom labels.** Five free fields you can use for anything: margin band, season, best sellers, clearance, model year. These are how you get the feed to reflect the business, so you can bid differently on high-margin products or push clearance stock harder.

## Images and price

Use the main product image on a clean background with no text or watermarks. The image is what people actually look at in the Shopping carousel, so a clear shot of the whole product beats a lifestyle photo cropped into a square.

Price and availability need to match the website exactly, and be updated often. If your stock changes daily, the feed needs to as well. Mismatches lead to disapprovals, and worse, to people clicking through for a product that's out of stock or more expensive than the ad said.

## The European changes

There's a bigger change happening at the moment that anyone advertising in Shopping in the UK and Europe should be aware of. In June the European Commission fined Google €2.42 billion for favouring its own comparison shopping service in search results. At the end of September Google responded by making Google Shopping operate as a separate unit in Europe and letting other comparison shopping services bid for space in the Shopping ads alongside it.

What this means in practice is still unclear. In principle, retailers can now reach the same ad slots through a third-party comparison service as well as through Google Shopping directly, sometimes at a different cost. Some of the comparison services are already pitching this hard. I'm going to watch it for a couple of months before changing anything, and I'd be cautious of anyone promising big savings until there's some real data. The feed fundamentals don't change either way; whichever route you use, the quality of your product data is what gets you shown.

## Where I'd start

If you only have a day, spend it on titles for your top hundred products by revenue, fix any missing GTINs, and check the Merchant Center diagnostics for errors. In my experience that's where most of the quick gains are. The rest can be done over the following weeks.
