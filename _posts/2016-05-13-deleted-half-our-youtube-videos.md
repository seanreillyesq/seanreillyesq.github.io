Our YouTube channel had been running for years, and like most channels that have run for years, it had turned into a bit of a junk drawer. Event clips nobody watched, old experiments, reviews with thumbnails that matched nothing else, the odd thing someone uploaded once and forgot about. This spring I went through the lot, and we deleted or unlisted about half of it.

Deleting content you paid to make feels wrong, and it took some arguing to get everyone comfortable with it. The sunk cost is right there in front of you; real money and real time went into some of those videos, and the instinct is to keep them because they exist. That instinct is the thing you have to get past.

The case for cutting is simpler than it sounds:

- Clutter counts against you. Someone landing on the channel judges it by what they see, and a wall of low-view, off-brand clips reads as "nobody is looking after this".
- Your good work gets buried. On your own channel, your best videos are competing for attention with your worst.
- Browse and suggested reward focus. A channel with a clear subject is easier for YouTube to make sense of, and to recommend, than a sprawling one.
- A tidy channel just looks more professional, and people clock that even if they couldn't tell you why.

We unlisted rather than hard-deleted anything that still had inbound links or sat embedded on a page somewhere; there was no sense breaking things for the sake of tidiness. The genuinely dead weight came off entirely.

The channel felt better afterwards in a way I found hard to put a number against, which is uncomfortable for someone who likes numbers. Fewer videos, a clearer channel, and the stuff worth watching no longer hidden behind the stuff that wasn't. It's the sort of job that never quite finishes, though. Leave a channel alone and it fills back up with clutter inside a year, so it's already back on the list for next time.
