Search Console has been hard to read this month. Google has stacked three updates on top of each other:

- The **helpful content update**, which started on 25 August and finished on 9 September
- The **September 2022 core update**, which started on 12 September
- The **September 2022 product reviews update**, which started on 20 September

Both September updates were confirmed as finished on Monday. That's the best part of five weeks where something was nearly always rolling out, which makes it hard to say which update caused any particular movement.

## Why the overlap matters

In a normal month I'd compare the fortnight after an update with the fortnight before and get a reasonable read. This time the "before" for the core update was the tail end of the helpful content rollout, and the product reviews update started before the core update finished. Any single before-and-after comparison is measuring a blend.

On top of that, it's late September. Schools are back, the weather has changed, and for anyone in fashion or gifting, autumn demand is starting to build. Seasonal shifts are easy to mistake for algorithm effects at the best of times.

## How I'm trying to separate them

There's no clean way, but here's what I'm doing.

**Look at the dates day by day.** Plot daily clicks for each section of the site in Search Console and look for the step changes. A shift that starts on 12 or 13 September points to the core update. One that starts around the 20th or 21st and is concentrated on review content points to the product reviews update.

**Split by page type.** The product reviews update targets content that reviews or compares products: buying guides, "best of" lists, comparison pages. Ordinary product pages generally aren't what it's looking at, so if your buying guides moved and your product and category pages didn't, that's a strong hint.

**Check the queries, not just the pages.** A page can lose some queries and gain others in the same update. Search Console's query report for a single page often tells a more useful story than the page total.

**Compare with demand.** Google Trends for your main product categories, and branded search impressions, help separate "fewer people searching" from "fewer people finding us".

## What the product reviews guidance asks for

This is the one I think most ecommerce teams underrate. Google's guidance for review content has been fairly consistent since the first update last year, and the bar has gone up with each one. It asks for things like:

- Evidence that someone has actually used the product, such as original photos, measurements or test results
- Explanation of what sets a product apart from its competitors
- Pros and cons, not just praise
- Links to more than one seller, so the reader has a choice

That last one is uncomfortable for retailers writing buying guides on their own site. I don't think anyone expects a shop to link to its competitors. But it shows what Google is rewarding: content that would still be useful to a buyer who never purchases from you. A shop's buying guide that's really a sales page with a list attached is going to struggle against a specialist who has used the things.

The flip side is that retailers often know their products better than any affiliate site does. Returns data, the questions customers ask, which items run large or small, which fabric pills after three washes. That's the kind of first-hand detail the guidance is asking for, and it tends to sit in the customer service inbox rather than on the website.

## Where I've landed

Wait another week for the data to settle, then work through the steps above section by section. Don't try to reverse-engineer a single cause if the dates don't support one. And if buying guides took a hit, rewrite the most important few with real product knowledge rather than tweaking the lot.
