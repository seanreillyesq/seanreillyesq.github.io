Last week Instagram announced the change a lot of retailers have been waiting for. Ads on the platform are getting action buttons, "Shop Now", "Install Now", "Sign Up" and the like, and advertisers get targeting built on Facebook's data rather than the fairly blunt options available until now. Until this point the only clickable link most brands had on Instagram was the one in their profile, which is why every caption you've ever read ends with "link in bio".

For luxury brands I think this is mostly good news. It also raises a question I'm not sure everyone is asking.

## Why Instagram suits luxury in the first place

Of all the big social networks, Instagram is the one that looks like a luxury brand wants to look. It's image-first, the quality bar is relatively high, and there's no cluttered sidebar of other ads competing for attention. A well-shot photograph of a watch dial or a handbag sits in the feed between photos of someone's holiday and someone else's dinner, and it doesn't look out of place. On Facebook the same image fights with a wall of text, links and adverts for car insurance.

It's also where the audience is spending time, younger affluent buyers especially. Instagram passed 300 million monthly users at the end of last year. And for a category that sells on desire, a medium built entirely around looking at things is a natural fit.

The brands that do it well treat it like a magazine they publish themselves. Craftsmanship, behind-the-scenes, the process of making things, the places and people associated with the brand. Product shots, yes, but rarely the plain packshot you'd use on the website.

## What the new ads change

Until now, Instagram has been very good for building awareness and very hard to tie to sales. You could post beautiful things, gain followers and engagement, and then struggle to show anyone in finance what it was worth. Without a link there was no click, and without a click there was nothing for analytics to see.

Clickable ads change that. You can send people from a sponsored post directly to a product page and measure what happens next. With proper targeting you can reach people by interests and behaviours rather than spraying an image at a broad demographic and hoping. For anyone who has had to justify spending time on Instagram with a chart of follower growth, that's a big step.

## The caveat

The thing I'd be careful about is what the new formats do to the feel of the feed.

Part of the reason Instagram works for luxury is that it doesn't feel like shopping; people mostly open it to look at things. The moment the feed fills with "Shop Now" buttons on every third post, it starts to look like every other ad platform, and the quality that made it attractive starts to erode. Instagram seems aware of this and has been cautious with ads so far. Whether that caution survives the commercial pressure now it's part of Facebook, I don't know.

For a luxury brand specifically there's also the question of whether a "Shop Now" button is the right call to action at all. Most people seeing a £5,000 item on Instagram are not going to buy it from their phone in the next five minutes. They might save it, remember it, mention it to someone, visit a store. If the campaign is judged purely on direct sales from the click it will probably look poor, even if it got exactly the right people interested.

## How I'd approach it

A few thoughts, for what they're worth:

- **Keep the organic feed as it is.** The brand's own posts should stay the curated magazine. Paid placements are a separate thing.
- **Make the ads look like good posts.** The paid content that works on Instagram is the content that would have worked organically anyway. If it looks like a banner ad, people scroll straight past.
- **Use the click for something useful.** A product page, yes, but also a story about the product, a booking page, a store finder. Not every luxury journey ends at a checkout.
- **Measure over a longer window.** Look at assisted conversions and returning visitors, not just last-click sales.

I'm looking forward to testing it properly once the new formats are widely available, mainly for the targeting.
