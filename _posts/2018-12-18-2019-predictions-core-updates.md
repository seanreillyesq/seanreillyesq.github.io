I'm writing down a few guesses about the year ahead, mostly so I can be honest with myself about what I got wrong twelve months from now. Looking back over 2018, the story in search that stuck with me most was how often Google changed things, and how little it told anyone about each change.

## 1. Broad core updates become the normal rhythm

Google confirmed broad core updates in March, April and August this year, plus plenty of unconfirmed movement in between. Each time the official line was much the same: nothing specific to fix, focus on making great content, read the rater guidelines.

I expect 2019 to bring more of the same, probably three or four confirmed updates and a lot of noise around each. That has a practical consequence. The old way of doing SEO, where you waited for a named update, worked out what it targeted and adjusted, doesn't fit well any more. When an update has no single target, there's nothing to reverse-engineer. The sites that do well across several updates in a row tend to be the ones that would look good to a human reviewer anyway.

For anyone reporting to a board or a client, I'd start setting expectations now. Rankings will move more often, sometimes for reasons nobody can fully explain, and a drop after a core update isn't necessarily a penalty. I wrote more about the [Medic update](/medic-update-eat-ymyl-20180814/) in August, which is the one that pushed E-A-T into everyone's vocabulary.

## 2. Mobile-first indexing covers most of the web

The [rollout started in March](/mobile-first-indexing-rollout-begins-20180328/), and Google has been moving sites across in batches ever since. By the end of 2019 I'd expect the large majority of sites to be indexed this way, with the stragglers being the ones whose mobile versions are still missing content. If you're running a separate mobile site with trimmed-down pages, this is the year to sort it out.

## 3. Speed keeps creeping up the agenda

The [Speed Update](/google-speed-update-ecommerce-20180717/) in July only affects the slowest pages, and I don't expect Google to dramatically increase its weight in 2019. But Google keeps building better tools for measuring real-user performance, and I'd guess it will find more ways to surface that data to site owners. Ecommerce teams that haven't looked at their mobile product pages lately should, if only for the conversion rate.

## 4. The first big GDPR fines land

Seven months after GDPR came in, there has been no huge fine against a big tech company yet. Regulators have been building up casework and the complaints have been piling up, particularly about consent for advertising data. I think 2019 is the year we see the first big penalty against a household name, and I suspect it will involve consent rather than a data breach. If that happens, I'd expect cookie banners to change quickly.

## 5. Voice search stays overhyped

I've been hearing that voice search will change everything for a few years now. Smart speakers are selling well, and I'm sure people use them, but mostly for music, timers, weather and quick questions. I don't see much evidence of people buying things by voice in any meaningful volume, especially anything considered or expensive. I'd keep an eye on it and not much more.

## 6. Instagram becomes a proper shop window

With [shopping tags now in Stories and Explore](/instagram-shopping-luxury-brands-20181009/), I expect Instagram to push harder into commerce next year, and probably to start testing ways of buying without leaving the app. For brands that sell visual products, that would make it a sales channel in its own right.

## And one for the luxury side

This one is less a prediction than a hope. I'd like to see more heritage and luxury brands treat their websites as their best shop rather than an afterthought. Customers are clearly comfortable spending serious money online now, provided the experience gives them confidence. I suspect the ones that invest in product information, photography and service will start taking customers from the ones that don't.

I'll check back on these next December.
