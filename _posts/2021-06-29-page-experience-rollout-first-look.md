Back in January I wrote about [Core Web Vitals and the Page Experience update](/core-web-vitals-page-experience-update-explained-20210128/) ahead of what was then a May launch. In April Google pushed it back to mid-June and said the rollout would be gradual, running until the end of August. It started on 15 June. Two weeks in, I can't point to anything it has done.

The update may well matter in time. The timing just makes it nearly impossible to read right now.

## Why it's hard to see anything

**It's being phased in on purpose.** Google said page experience would play only a small role at first and build up over the summer. A signal that's being turned up slowly won't produce a sharp line on a chart.

**There's a core update in the way.** The June core update started on 2 June, and Google has already said a second one is due in July because some of the changes weren't ready. Core updates move rankings far more than a page experience tweak ever will. Any movement over these weeks is going to be blamed on whichever update the person doing the blaming happens to be thinking about.

**Seasonality and reopening.** Shops reopened in April, restrictions eased further in May, and people are spending more time outdoors and less time browsing. Search demand in plenty of retail categories looks different from a year ago for reasons that have nothing to do with Google.

**Field data lags.** Core Web Vitals scores are based on a rolling 28 days of Chrome user data. If you fixed something in May, Search Console is still partly showing you the old version of your site.

## What Google has said it is

To be fair to Google, it's been consistent. Page experience is one signal among hundreds. Great content on a slow page can still outrank weak content on a fast one. Where two pages are similar on relevance, experience may tip it. Google also added a Page Experience report to Search Console in April, which pulls Core Web Vitals, mobile usability, HTTPS and the rest into one view. It's a decent at-a-glance summary.

## What I'm watching

Over the summer I'm keeping an eye on a few things rather than staring at overall traffic.

- **Pages that moved from "poor" to "good" in Search Console** compared with similar pages that didn't. That's about as close to a controlled comparison as you'll get without a lab.
- **Mobile versus desktop.** The update applies to mobile results first, so if anything shows up, it should show up there before desktop.
- **Conversion rate on the pages we've sped up.** This is the number I'd have cared about regardless. If faster pages convert better, the work has paid for itself whatever the ranking effect.
- **Templates rather than single pages.** Search Console groups similar URLs together, so a fix to the product page template should show up as a whole block of URLs changing status at once. If only a handful move, the fix probably didn't reach every page that uses the template.
- **Competitors' scores.** If everyone in a niche is equally slow, being slightly faster isn't going to separate you much. If a competitor has done the work and you haven't, that's worth knowing.

## A small worry

The industry has spent six months making Core Web Vitals sound like a cliff edge. My concern is the opposite reaction once rollout ends without drama: someone decides it was all hype, and the performance budget gets cut. The conversion argument for a fast product page was there before any of this and doesn't depend on what Google does with it.

The rollout is due to finish at the end of August, and by September there should be a cleaner run of data to compare.
