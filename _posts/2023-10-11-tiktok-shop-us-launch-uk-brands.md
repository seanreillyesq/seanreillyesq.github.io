TikTok officially launched TikTok Shop in the US on 12 September, and it has dominated the ecommerce newsletters I read ever since. Here in the UK it isn't new. Sellers have been able to use it since 2021, though it has never had quite the attention it's getting now that the US is involved.

This is my attempt to think through whether UK brands should take it seriously. I haven't run a TikTok Shop myself, so treat this as the questions I'd ask rather than advice from someone who has.

## What it is

In short, shopping inside the app. Products appear in videos, in live streams and in a shop tab. Customers can buy without leaving TikTok, and creators can earn commission for promoting products from sellers they've never met, through an affiliate programme. Sellers list products, handle fulfilment (or use TikTok's in some markets), and TikTok takes a fee on each sale.

That last part, the affiliate model, is the thing that makes it different from a shoppable Instagram post. Your product can end up in front of millions of people because a creator decided it would make a good video, without you commissioning anything.

## Why it's tempting

The audience is huge and it's spending hours a day in the app. Buying is quick, and impulse purchases are the whole point. For a brand selling something visual, cheap enough to buy on a whim, and easy to demonstrate in fifteen seconds, it could be a very efficient way to sell.

TikTok also seems to be spending heavily to make the US launch work, with discounts and subsidies for buyers and sellers. When a platform is paying to grow, the early sellers sometimes get a better deal than anyone who comes later.

## The questions I'd ask first

**Does your product suit the format?** Gadgets, beauty, cleaning products, novelty items and cheap fashion do well in short video. A £400 handmade leather bag or a bespoke shirt is a harder sell in a scrolling feed that rewards impulse. Having spent years in luxury ecommerce, I'm sceptical about how well considered purchases fit.

**What does it do to your pricing?** A lot of what sells on TikTok Shop sells on discount. If you're building a brand that rarely discounts, joining a marketplace where everything is "flash sale" changes how people see you. That's harder to put a number on than the fees.

**Who owns the customer?** You get an order, but the relationship lives on TikTok. Marketing consent, repeat purchase and the customer's history mostly stay on the platform. That's true of Amazon too, and plenty of brands decide it's a price worth paying. I'd just want someone to have actually decided it.

**Can your operations keep up?** A video that takes off can mean thousands of orders in a day. If your stock, warehouse and customer service can't handle a spike, you could turn a lucky moment into a pile of late deliveries and bad reviews.

**Who's the face of it?** Creator content needs managing. Affiliates will describe your product in their own words, which is the appeal, but also means claims you'd never make yourself. Someone needs to keep an eye on it.

## My view, for now

For most UK brands I'd treat TikTok Shop as a test, not a strategy. Pick a handful of products that suit the format, work out the margin properly once fees, affiliate commission and any discounting are taken off, and give it a defined period with a clear measure of success.

If the product doesn't suit it, I'd sit it out and put the effort into the website and email list you control.

I'll be interested to see how the US launch looks after its first Black Friday. That should tell us a lot more than the launch headlines have.
