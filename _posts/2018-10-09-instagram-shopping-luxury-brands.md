Instagram <a href="https://business.instagram.com/blog/announcing-shopping-in-instagram-stories-and-explore" target="_blank" rel="noopener">announced last month</a> that shopping tags are rolling out to Stories globally, alongside a new Shopping channel in Explore. Shoppable posts in the main feed have been available to UK businesses since March, so this fills in the gap. A brand can now tag products in most of the places people actually look on Instagram.

For brands that sell things people want to look at closely, it's one of the more useful changes Instagram has made for a while.

## How it works

You tag products in a post the same way you'd tag a person. Followers see a small shopping bag icon on the image; tapping it shows the product name and price, and another tap opens a product page inside Instagram with a link through to your website to buy. Checkout still happens on your own site, which I'm quite glad of. You keep the customer relationship and the data.

In Stories, a product sticker does the same job. In Explore, the new Shopping channel shows shoppable posts from brands you follow and some you don't, which is the more interesting part. It's a route for new people to find you while they're already in a browsing frame of mind.

## Setting it up

The requirements aren't complicated, but they take a while to get through:

1. A **business profile** on Instagram, connected to a Facebook Page.
2. A **product catalogue** in Facebook's Business Manager. If you are on Shopify this can be synced directly, which is far easier than building one by hand.
3. Your products need to be **physical goods** that meet Facebook's commerce policies.
4. **Approval.** Instagram reviews the account before the shopping features switch on. It can take anything from a few days to a couple of weeks, and from what I have read the feedback when it is declined is not always clear.

Once approved, the option to tag products appears when you create a post. You can tag up to five products on a single image.

## What makes sense for luxury

The obvious risk for a premium brand is turning a beautiful feed into a catalogue. People follow luxury brands on Instagram for the craft, the materials, the workshop, the people. If every post becomes a price tag, I think you lose the reason they followed in the first place.

So my approach is to tag sparingly, and tag the right posts:

- **Detail shots.** A close-up of a stitched edge or a cloth is exactly the moment someone wonders "what is that and what does it cost?". The tag answers it without spoiling the picture.
- **Styled outfits.** Tagging two or three items in a single look works well, and it suits how people actually shop for clothes and accessories.
- **Stories for new arrivals.** Stories are temporary and informal anyway, so a product sticker on a new piece feels natural rather than pushy.
- **Not the brand content.** Workshop and heritage posts work better without tags. They're there to build trust, and a price tag on a workshop shot just looks odd.

## Measuring it

Instagram's own insights show taps on product tags and clicks through to the website. I'd add UTM parameters to the product URLs in the catalogue so the traffic shows up properly in Google Analytics, otherwise it gets lumped into general Instagram referrals.

I wouldn't expect it to drive huge direct sales straight away. For a high-value item, the person who taps a tag is often researching rather than ready to buy. They might come back later via search or email. Last-click reporting will undersell it, as it does with most social activity.

## Worth doing

For a brand with a strong Instagram presence and a product that photographs well, I can't see much reason not to switch this on. It costs nothing beyond setup time and saves an interested follower hunting for the product on your website.
