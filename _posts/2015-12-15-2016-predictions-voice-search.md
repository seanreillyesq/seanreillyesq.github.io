Last December I wrote [a few predictions for 2015](/2015-digital-marketing-predictions-20141217/) and promised to check how many I got wrong. So, briefly, before making a fresh set of mistakes.

Mobile stopping being a separate conversation: about right, and Google did a lot of the work for me by [putting a date on it](/google-mobile-friendly-update-april-20150227/) in February. In May it confirmed that more Google searches now happen on mobile than on desktop in ten countries, including the US and Japan. Paid social becoming the default: also about right, helped by Instagram finally opening up proper ads. Secure sites becoming normal: getting there, more slowly than I expected. Content marketing getting a reckoning: partly. Plenty of people still seem to believe that more is better.

I also said 2015 would be a year of change for me personally. It was, with a move into luxury ecommerce in the spring.

For 2016, most of what I'm thinking about comes back to one thing: how people talk to their devices.

## Voice search grows up

Voice search has existed for years without many people using it in public. Siri arrived in 2011, Google has had voice search on Android and in its app for even longer, and most people tried it once, got a strange answer, and went back to typing.

I think that's starting to change.

**Recognition has got much better.** The jump in accuracy over the last couple of years is noticeable. Speaking a query and getting the right words back used to be a coin toss. Now it mostly just works, even with a regional accent.

**It's being built into everything.** Microsoft put Cortana into Windows 10 this summer, so it's now sitting on millions of desktops. Apple and Google keep pushing their assistants further into their phones. And in the US, Amazon's Echo, a speaker you simply talk to, went on general sale this year. It isn't available here yet, but I'd be surprised if it stays that way for long.

**The answers have got better.** Google increasingly answers a question directly rather than just listing links, which is exactly what you need if you're asking out loud and can't see a screen.

## What changes when people speak

Typed searches tend to be short and clipped. "Weather Canterbury". "Rolex Submariner price". Spoken ones are longer and phrased as questions. "What's the weather going to be like in Canterbury tomorrow?" "How much is a second-hand Rolex Submariner worth?"

That has a few knock-on effects I'd expect to become more obvious next year:

- **Question-shaped content wins.** Pages that clearly answer a specific question, in plain language, are the ones a voice assistant can use. I'd expect FAQ-style content and good, direct answers near the top of a page to matter more.
- **Local intent increases.** A lot of voice searches happen on the move: "near me", "open now", "how do I get to". Getting the basics right in Google My Business, and keeping opening hours and addresses accurate everywhere, matters more if someone's asking a phone in the car.
- **Keyword research gets harder.** We don't yet get much data separating spoken queries from typed ones. I'd expect to be squinting at longer, more conversational phrases in search query reports and working backwards.
- **Being the answer matters more than ranking.** On a screen, position three still gets clicks. When an assistant reads a result out, it usually only reads one.

This links straight back to [RankBrain](/rankbrain-long-tail-intent-20151030/). A system designed to understand unfamiliar, conversational queries arrives just as more queries become unfamiliar and conversational. I don't think that's a coincidence.

## A few others, briefly

**Ad blocking becomes a mainstream worry.** Apple allowed content blockers in Safari on iOS 9 in September, and the publishers I read are visibly nervous. I expect more of the conversation next year to be about ads that people don't want to block, and a lot of hand-wringing in the meantime.

**Faster mobile pages get a push.** Google announced the Accelerated Mobile Pages project in October, aimed at news articles that load almost instantly on phones. It's publisher-focused for now, but I'd be surprised if speed doesn't become a bigger theme for everyone.

**Video moves into the feed.** Facebook has been pushing native video hard all year, autoplaying in the feed. I expect brands to keep shifting budget there, and to keep arguing about what counts as a "view".

## The one I'm least sure about

My voice search prediction could easily be early. People still feel self-conscious talking to their phones in public, and that kind of habit takes a while to shift. It might be 2017 or 2018 before it changes behaviour at any real scale.

But writing content that answers real questions is worth doing anyway, so I'd rather start now than catch up later. Happy Christmas, and I'll check back in on these next December.
