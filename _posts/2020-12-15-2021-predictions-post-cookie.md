I nearly didn't write a predictions post this year. Anything I'd have written last December would have been wrong within three months, and I'm not sure I'm better placed now. But writing them down is still useful, if only to see later what I was worried about.

Most of my guesses this year are about the same theme. Tracking is getting harder, and 2021 is when a lot of marketers feel it for the first time.

## 1. Apple's tracking prompt lands, and Facebook advertisers feel it

Apple announced at WWDC in June that iOS 14 would make apps ask permission before tracking users across other apps and websites. It then delayed the requirement to early next year after pushback from developers and advertisers. Facebook has been unusually public in its objections, which tells you how much it expects to lose.

I expect this to be the privacy change that ecommerce teams notice most in 2021, more than anything happening in browsers. If most iPhone users decline tracking, which I think they will, then the conversion data flowing back to Facebook and Instagram ads gets thinner. Attribution in Ads Manager will get worse, targeting will get blunter, and anyone who relies heavily on Facebook for acquisition should expect results to look worse even if nothing has changed in real sales.

## 2. Chrome's cookie deadline gets closer, and the replacement is still unclear

In January Google said Chrome would phase out third-party cookies within two years. Safari already blocks them, and Firefox blocks the ones from known trackers by default. Google's alternatives under the Privacy Sandbox banner, including FLoC for interest-based advertising, are still proposals being argued about in standards groups.

I don't expect third-party cookies to disappear from Chrome in 2021. I do expect the arguments about the replacements to get louder, and the ad industry to spend the year in some confusion. The practical lesson stays the same: any marketing that depends on following individuals around the web through third-party cookies has a limited life.

## 3. First-party data becomes the main asset

Following from the first two, I think 2021 is the year more ecommerce teams start treating their own customer data as their most valuable marketing asset. That means email lists, customer accounts, purchase histories and the consent to use them.

For heritage brands, this is good news. Their strength has always been loyal customers who buy directly and come back. Brands that have spent this year adding new direct customers, which many did during lockdown, have something valuable now, provided they look after it properly.

## 4. Page experience becomes a ranking factor

Google has confirmed that its page experience update, which brings Core Web Vitals into ranking, will arrive in May 2021. I don't think it'll be dramatic. Content relevance will still matter far more, and Google has said as much. But it gives ecommerce teams a clear deadline to sort out slow mobile pages and layout shift, and I expect plenty of developer time to go on it in the first half of the year.

## 5. Analytics gets messier before it gets better

Between consent banners, Safari's cookie restrictions, the iOS 14 changes and the new [Google Analytics 4](/google-analytics-4-launch-20201022/), the numbers in analytics tools are going to be harder to trust next year. I expect a lot of time to go into explaining why different tools disagree. Teams that agree now on which numbers they'll treat as the source of truth, and why, will save themselves a lot of meetings.

## 6. Brexit friction shows up in ecommerce

The transition period ends on 31 December, and whether or not there's a trade deal, shipping to EU customers changes on 1 January. Customs declarations, possible duties and VAT on arrival are going to catch out some UK brands and frustrate some European customers. My guess is that early 2021 brings stories of parcels stuck at borders and customers refusing deliveries with unexpected charges, and that some smaller UK brands pause EU sales while they sort it out.

## 7. Online keeps most of what it gained

Finally, the safest one. Whatever happens with vaccines and restrictions, I expect a large part of the shift online to stick in 2021. Shops will reopen and some customers will return, but most of the people who set up accounts and saved their card details this year will keep using them.

I'll check these against reality this time next year.
