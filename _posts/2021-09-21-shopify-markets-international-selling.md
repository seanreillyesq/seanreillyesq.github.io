Shopify opened Markets in early access last week, with a wider rollout to merchants promised over the coming months. It was first announced at Shopify's Unite event in June, and the pitch is simple: sell into multiple countries from a single store, with local currencies, local domains or subfolders, translated content, and duties handled at checkout.

For a British brand trying to sell abroad after Brexit, that list reads like someone at Shopify has been reading the complaints.

## The problem it's trying to solve

Until now, serious international selling on Shopify has often meant running several stores. A UK store, an EU store, a US store, each with its own theme, catalogue, apps and inventory sync. That works, and plenty of brands do it, but it's a lot of duplication. Change a product description and you're changing it three times. Run a promotion and someone has to remember which store it's live on.

The alternative was a single store with a currency converter bolted on, which handles the pricing display and not much else.

Markets sits in between. One store, but with settings per country or group of countries: currency, pricing, language, domain and which products are available.

## Where I think it helps

**Duties and taxes at checkout.** This is the one I care most about. Since January, EU customers buying from the UK can be hit by import VAT and duty on delivery, and I wrote about how much that complicates [luxury returns](/luxury-returns-after-brexit-20210323/) back in March. Showing the full landed cost at checkout, so the customer pays once and isn't ambushed by a courier, removes one of the worst parts of post-Brexit cross-border selling. From what I've read it isn't available on every plan, so check that applies to you.

**Proper international SEO structure.** Country subfolders or domains, with hreflang handled by the platform, is a lot better than a single URL with prices that flip depending on IP address. Google can see and index each market's version, and it's much less likely to show a US shopper a page priced in sterling.

**Pricing control by market.** A heritage product priced for the UK doesn't automatically make sense in dollars or euros after conversion. Being able to set rounded prices per market, rather than €87.43, looks a lot more professional.

**Less duplication.** One catalogue, one theme, one set of apps. For a small team that's the real saving.

## What it won't fix

**Logistics.** Shopify can show the right price and collect the duties. It can't make the parcel arrive faster or make a returned item clear customs back into the UK any more smoothly.

**Local marketing.** Being able to sell in Germany doesn't mean anyone in Germany has heard of you. Paid search, local PR, partners and customer service in the right language all still need doing, and that's where most of the cost of entering a market sits.

**Translation quality.** The platform gives you somewhere to put translated content. It doesn't write it. Machine-translating product copy for a luxury brand is a quick way to sound cheap.

**Early-access caveats.** It's new. I'd expect gaps, especially around apps that haven't caught up yet. Anyone running a well-oiled multi-store setup shouldn't rip it out based on a launch announcement.

## Who I'd suggest looks at it

If you're a UK brand on Shopify, already getting some international orders on a single store, and wondering whether to go multi-store, I'd hold off on building extra stores and look at Markets first. If you already run separate country stores with different teams or ranges, I'd watch how it develops for a few months.

I'll be reading the documentation more closely over the next few weeks, mainly to see how the duties calculation handles products with mixed origins and materials. That's where the detail usually bites.
