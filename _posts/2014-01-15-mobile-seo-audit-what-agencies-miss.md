More than a third of the visits I look at now come from a phone, and on some sites it is past half. That number keeps climbing every quarter, and yet the average site audit I read still treats mobile as a footnote at the end. A paragraph that says "the site is responsive" and then moves on.

Responsive is a starting point, not an answer. A responsive theme tells you the layout reflows. It says nothing about whether the page is usable on a train with two bars of signal.

Here is what I actually check when I sit down with a site and my phone.

**Speed on a real connection.** Desktop tools test on fat pipes. I throttle the connection to something closer to 3G and see how long the thing takes to become useful. Not "loaded" in the technical sense, but the point where I can read and tap something. Big hero images and a stack of blocking scripts turn a two second page into an eight second one, and people leave well before then.

**What Googlebot sees versus what I see.** If a site serves a stripped separate mobile version on an m-dot subdomain, the two can drift apart badly. Content that exists on desktop can vanish on mobile without anyone noticing. I fetch both and compare. Webmaster Tools has a Fetch as Google option that is worth ten minutes of anyone's time here.

**Tap targets and forms.** This sounds trivial until you try to complete a checkout with your thumb. Links crammed together, a phone number that is not click to call, a form with a dropdown of 200 countries and no keyboard support. None of that shows up in a crawl. You find it by using the site badly, on purpose, the way a distracted person would.

**Redirects and faulty equivalence.** A classic one is every mobile URL redirecting to the mobile homepage rather than the mobile version of the requested page. Someone shares a product link, a phone user taps it, and lands on the front page with no idea where the product went. Crawlers flag the redirect but not the fact that it points somewhere useless.

**Interstitials.** App install banners and full screen overlays that cover the content the moment you arrive. Annoying on desktop, close to hostile on a small screen.

None of this needs an expensive tool. It needs someone willing to hold a phone and actually use the site for ten minutes with the patience of a normal human being, which is to say not much. Most audits skip that step because it does not fit neatly in a spreadsheet. That is exactly why it is worth doing.
