This is more of a thinking-out-loud post than a useful guide. I have been turning over a question a lot lately, the one plenty of people in marketing hit at some point: agency or in-house? I have not decided anything. I just find writing it down helps me see what I actually think rather than what I assume I think.

The pull of agency work is variety. You touch a lot of different businesses, sectors and problems in a short space of time. You are exposed to more in a year than you might be in three somewhere else, and if you get bored easily that is a real draw. You are surrounded by other specialists, so you learn quickly and you always have someone to ask.

The frustration is that you are always slightly on the outside. You do good work, you hand it over, and you rarely get to live with the results the way the client does. You are pulled between accounts, and the deep, patient work that a brand really benefits from is hard to do when your attention is split across several of them. You care, but it is a different kind of caring than owning the thing outright.

In-house looks very different from where I sit. One brand, one audience, one set of problems you get to actually stay with. You can build something over months and years, see it compound, get to know the product and the customers properly rather than skimming. That depth appeals to me more the longer I do this. There is something to being able to say a result is yours, start to finish, rather than a contribution to someone else's.

The obvious risk is narrowing. One business, one sector, and if it is not moving or not investing, you can stall. You lose the constant churn of new problems that keeps agency work sharp. And you take on all the internal politics and constraints that clients usually shield the agency from, which is its own education.

Neither is better in the abstract; it depends on what you happen to want more of at a given stage. Some people thrive on the breadth and speed of agency life and would be bored rigid inside one company. Others would rather own a single thing properly and stay with it long enough to see it through. I can feel myself pulled towards the second of those, though leaning is not the same as deciding, and I have talked myself out of firm conclusions before now.

So no neat resolution here. I am putting the question down on paper mostly to work out which of those trade-offs actually matters to me, as opposed to which one sounds better when I describe it to someone else. If you have made the jump either direction, I would be interested to hear how it landed once the novelty wore off.
