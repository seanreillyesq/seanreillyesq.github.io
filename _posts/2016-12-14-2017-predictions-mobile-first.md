Every December I write down what I think the next year holds, mostly so I can be honest with myself about it twelve months later. This year the list is heavily tilted towards mobile, because Google has spent most of 2016 telling us, fairly loudly, where it's heading.

## Mobile-first indexing

The big one. Last month Google said it has started experimenting with mobile-first indexing: using the mobile version of a page as the main version for indexing and ranking, instead of the desktop one. At the moment, if your mobile site is a cut-down version of your desktop site, Google mostly judges you on the fuller desktop content. Once this rolls out, it'll judge you on what a phone visitor sees.

Google has been careful to call it an experiment and hasn't given a date. My guess is that it rolls out gradually and takes well over a year to reach most sites. But I'd treat 2017 as the year to get ready for it.

For ecommerce, the checks are fairly clear:

- Is the product content on mobile the same as on desktop, including specifications, descriptions and reviews?
- Is structured data present on the mobile pages, not just the desktop ones?
- If you run a separate m-dot site, are the canonical and alternate tags set up properly between the two?

Responsive sites are in the best position here. Separate mobile sites built years ago and not touched much are the ones I'd worry about.

## The interstitial penalty

From 10 January, Google will start treating pages that throw intrusive pop-ups at mobile visitors arriving from search as less useful. Full-screen newsletter overlays, "download our app" screens and similar. Legally required things like cookie notices and age checks are exempt, as are small banners that don't take over the screen.

In May I said I wouldn't bet on newsletter pop-ups being left alone, and here we are. Plenty of ecommerce sites rely on a pop-up for email sign-ups, so I'd expect a lot of hurried redesigns over Christmas.

## Chrome and HTTPS

Chrome 56, due in January, will mark HTTP pages that collect passwords or card details as "Not secure". Google has said that's the first step and that eventually all HTTP pages will get the warning. For any ecommerce site still running checkout or login over plain HTTP, that's an urgent fix. For everyone else, Google clearly wants every page on HTTPS, and I'd plan the move for 2017 rather than wait for the warning.

## AMP spreads further

When I wrote about [AMP in February](/google-amp-luxury-ecommerce-20160226/) it was only being shown for news. From September, AMP pages started showing in ordinary mobile results too. I still don't think it suits rich product pages, but I'd expect more retailers to try it on category and content pages in 2017, and some ecommerce platforms to offer it as a built-in option.

## Paid search gets more mobile and more crowded

Expanded text ads will be the only format you can create in AdWords from the end of January, and with only four ads at the top of results, competition for those slots on mobile is fierce. I expect cost per click on mobile to keep climbing, and more advertisers to look seriously at call extensions and location extensions as phones take a bigger share of searches.

## Social becomes more of a shop

Instagram started testing shoppable posts with a handful of US brands last month. If that works, I'd expect it to reach more brands and more countries during 2017. For luxury brands that raises an awkward question about how salesy a feed can get before people stop following it.

## GDPR is coming, slowly

The EU's General Data Protection Regulation was agreed this year and applies from May 2018. That feels a long way off, and it'll be easy to leave it until 2018. I think that's a mistake. Consent for email marketing, how customer data is stored, and how long it's kept will all need looking at, and none of that is quick in a business with years of customer records spread across several systems. Whatever happens with Brexit, I expect UK businesses dealing with EU customers will need to comply.

## Voice keeps growing

Google Home is out in the US, Echo is selling here, and I'd expect Google's device to reach the UK sometime in 2017. I still think the practical work is mostly about featured snippets, as I wrote [in October](/voice-search-optimisation-20161027/), and I'd keep the investment modest until there's some way to measure it.

I'll look back at this list next December and see how much of it was right.
