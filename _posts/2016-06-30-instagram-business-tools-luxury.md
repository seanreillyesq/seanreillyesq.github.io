Instagram announced last week that it has passed 500 million monthly users. In the same few weeks it has been rolling out the algorithmic feed it trailed in March, and it has announced business profiles, with a contact button and analytics, coming to accounts over the next few months. Taken together, that's Instagram turning into a proper business platform, and it changes the deal for brands that have been using it as a free shop window.

## The feed

The reverse-chronological feed is going. Instead, Instagram will show you posts in the order it thinks you'll care about most, based on who you interact with and what you engage with. It's the same journey Facebook went on a few years ago, and anyone who managed a Facebook page through 2014 will remember how that ended for organic reach.

I'm not predicting the same collapse overnight. Instagram has said posts won't disappear, only move around. But the principle is the same: the platform decides what gets seen, engagement drives that decision, and over time the easiest way to guarantee being seen will be to pay for it.

For luxury brands this is a bigger change than it sounds, because Instagram is where a lot of them have put most of their social effort. Watches photograph beautifully. The enthusiast community on Instagram is huge and properly knowledgeable. It's been the one social channel where posting good pictures consistently was enough.

## Business profiles

Business profiles are the other half. You'll be able to mark an account as a business, add a contact button so someone can call, email or get directions straight from the profile, and see analytics on how your posts and followers behave. At the moment the only route to most of that is third-party tools of varying quality, so the native version is welcome.

The catch, and it's hardly a secret, is that a business profile makes it very easy for Instagram to show you how much more reach you'd get with a bit of spend behind your posts. That's fine. It's a business and it needs to make money. I'd just rather go in with my eyes open.

## What I'd change, and what I wouldn't

I'm a little wary of social for its own sake. In my experience, organic social is very hard to tie to sales, and I'd treat anyone who tells you otherwise with some caution. I still think Instagram is worth the effort, I just want us to agree what it's for.

For a luxury business I'd see its job as mostly about brand and community: showing the product properly, giving enthusiasts something worth following, and being there when someone is in the research phase and looks you up. That job doesn't change because the feed is algorithmic. What changes is how you get the content in front of people.

So, practically:

- **Post less, better.** If engagement now decides visibility, a weak post may also count against how the account is treated in future, though nobody outside Instagram knows how much. I'd rather post three strong images a week than something every day for the sake of it.
- **Get the analytics working early.** When business profiles reach UK accounts, switch over and start building a baseline straight away. You want to know what normal looks like before you change anything.
- **Budget a little for paid, and test it like any other channel.** Instagram ads have been open to all advertisers for a while now. Treat them as a measurable media buy with the same scrutiny as display, and don't assume a pretty picture will convert because it got likes.
- **Don't buy followers or engagement.** It's always been a bad idea. With an engagement-ranked feed, a big audience that never interacts actively hurts you.

## The bit I'll be watching

The thing I can't call yet is whether Instagram moves towards letting people buy directly from posts. At the moment the platform is still very closed about links: one in your bio and that's largely it, unless you pay. If that loosens, it changes the case for Instagram from brand building to something much closer to a sales channel, and luxury brands will have some interesting decisions to make about how commercial they want their feed to look.

For now, I'm keeping our approach close to what it was, with fewer, better posts, and a small amount of paid spend that we'll measure properly.
