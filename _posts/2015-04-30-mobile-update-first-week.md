The mobile-friendly update started rolling out on Tuesday 21 April. By the reaction in the run-up, you'd have expected the search results to be unrecognisable by now. Somebody christened it "Mobilegeddon", the name stuck, and the last few weeks have been full of agencies sending clients worried emails and tool vendors publishing countdowns.

Nine days in, the honest summary is that not very much seems to have happened. Not yet, anyway.

## What we're seeing so far

The rank tracking tools that measure volatility in Google's results have shown movement, but nothing like the earthquake people were braced for. Most of the sites I keep an eye on, mobile-friendly or otherwise, have moved a little or not at all. A few non-friendly pages have slipped down mobile results for competitive terms. Nothing I've seen looks like the sort of cliff edge Panda and Penguin produced for the sites they hit.

There are reports of individual sites taking real hits, and I don't doubt some of them. But the widespread collapse hasn't materialised.

## Why it might look quieter than expected

I can think of a few reasons, and I suspect it's a combination.

**It's still rolling out.** Google said up front that the change would take a week or more to roll out fully, and since it's evaluated page by page as pages get recrawled, a less-visited page might not be reassessed for a while.

**A lot of sites fixed themselves.** Two months' notice and the word "significant" from Google are a powerful motivator. I'd guess a large number of sites that would have failed in February passed by April, which is presumably what Google wanted all along.

**It's one signal among hundreds.** Relevance still wins. A page that isn't mobile-friendly but is clearly the best answer to a query isn't going to vanish. Where it should bite is in close contests, where two pages are similarly relevant and one works on a phone and the other doesn't.

**It only affects mobile results.** If you're checking rankings from a desktop, you won't see a thing. It's an easy mistake to make if your rank reports default to desktop, and it's probably behind some of the "damp squib" verdicts going round.

## What I'd do now

If your site passed the Mobile-Friendly Test before the 21st, carry on, and keep an eye on your mobile organic traffic in analytics rather than a rank tracker. Segment it by device and compare it with the same weeks before the update. That will tell you more than any industry-wide volatility chart.

If your site didn't pass and you haven't fixed it, I wouldn't take the quiet start as permission to leave it. The signal is in place and will keep working away as pages are recrawled, and there's no reason to think Google's interest in mobile stops here. The traffic split between phone and desktop is only going one way.

And if you've been tracking it, check the pages that failed rather than the site as a whole. Because it's page by page, a site-level average can hide a handful of important templates that have quietly dropped.

## On the hype

I don't think the build-up was entirely wasted, even if "Mobilegeddon" turns out to have been an overstatement. It got a lot of businesses to look at their sites on a phone for the first time in a while, and plenty of them didn't like what they saw.

But it does make me a bit wary about the next one. A lot of people spent money in a hurry this spring because of a nickname, and I'd rather see the same urgency applied to fixing mobile experiences because customers are using them, which has been the case for a couple of years now. I'll come back to this in a month or so when the data has settled.
