Shopify has been creeping up the market for a few years now, and Shopify Plus, its enterprise tier, comes up in almost every platform conversation I have with people running small and mid-sized brands. A couple of years ago the assumption was that serious ecommerce meant Magento, or something custom. That assumption has weakened a lot, partly because Magento 2 migrations turned out to be such hard work. I wrote about [planning one](/magento-2-migration-planning-20170620/) in 2017, and nothing I've heard since has made them sound easier.

So here's how I'd go about deciding whether Plus is right for a luxury or heritage brand. I don't have a verdict, because the answer depends heavily on the business, so this is more of a checklist.

## What you get

Plus is broadly the same Shopify that a small shop uses, with more headroom and a few extra tools. The parts that matter most for a brand like this:

- **Hosting, security and uptime are Shopify's problem.** For a small team that's the biggest benefit by a distance. No servers, no patching, no 2am phone calls during a sale.
- **Checkout customisation.** Standard Shopify locks the checkout down. Plus lets you change its look and, with Shopify Scripts, adjust things like discounts and shipping logic.
- **Automation.** Shopify Flow handles simple rules (tag this customer, alert someone when stock is low) without a developer, and Launchpad schedules sales and product releases.
- **Multiple stores.** Plus includes extra stores, which is one way to run separate sites for different regions or currencies.
- **Pricing** that starts at around $2,000 a month and scales with revenue. Expensive compared to standard Shopify, often cheap compared to the true cost of running Magento well.

## The questions I'd ask

**How complicated are your products?** This is the big one for luxury. A shirt with a choice of collar, cuff, fit and monogram is a lot of options. Shopify has a limit on variants and option types per product, and getting around it means apps or custom work. Made-to-measure or genuinely bespoke ordering is harder still. If that's a core part of the business, I'd want to see it working on a test store before committing.

**How international are you?** Selling in several currencies is getting easier on Shopify, but if you need genuinely different content, pricing and stock by region, you may end up running multiple stores, which means duplicate catalogues to keep in step. That's manageable, but it's work.

**What's your appetite for apps?** A lot of Shopify's flexibility comes from its app store. That's great until you have twenty apps, each with a monthly fee, each loading its own scripts, each with a support team in a different timezone. I'd count the apps you'd need on day one and price them properly.

**Does the shop matter?** For a brand with physical shops, stock and customer records ideally live in one place. Shopify's point of sale is decent for smaller stores and improving, but if you already have a retail system you're happy with, check how well they'd talk to each other.

**Who will look after it?** One reason Shopify suits small teams is that a non-developer can do most day-to-day work. If you're moving from a platform that needed an agency for every small change, that's a real gain in speed, and worth putting a value on.

## Where it fits less well

I'd be more cautious if the business needs heavily custom product configuration, complex B2B pricing alongside retail, or very specific checkout behaviour. None of these are impossible on Plus, but each one pushes you towards workarounds, and enough workarounds start to cancel out the reasons for choosing Shopify.

There's also a brand question. Out of the box, a lot of Shopify sites look rather alike. For a luxury brand, the theme needs proper design work so it doesn't look like every other store. That's a budget line people forget.

## How I'd test it

Before signing anything, I'd build a rough version of the five or six most awkward products on a development store, run a test order from each target country, and try the main daily tasks with whoever would actually do them. It takes about a week, and I'd trust it over the sales demos.

My hunch is that for a lot of small heritage brands Plus, or even standard Shopify, is now the sensible default. I'd still spend most of the evaluation time on the awkward products, the made-to-measure and the monogrammed, because they're the ones that won't fit a standard template.
